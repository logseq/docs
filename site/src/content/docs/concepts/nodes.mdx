**Node** is the term for what a page and a block have in common. They used to be different animals; in the DB version they behave almost identically.

## What every node can do

- Be referenced with `[[double brackets]]`.
- Be tagged — a node with a tag shows that tag's properties and joins its table.
- Have typed [properties](/concepts/properties) — set them in the same way, whether it's a page title or a nested block.
- Be embedded — the `/Node embed` command works on pages and blocks alike.
- Be favorited — zoom into a block to favorite it just like a page.
- Show `Linked References` and `Unlinked References` — on a page it appears at the bottom; on a block, zoom in to see it.
- Have an icon floating to its left, and collapse/expand with the arrow on hover.

Same keyboard shortcuts, too: property shortcuts like `Cmd-;` (toggle property display) work identically on pages and blocks.

## What's still page-only

- Pages appear in the **Pages** view and get created when you make a property, tag, or journal.
- Page names are unique *per tag* — `Apple #Company` and `Apple #Fruit` can coexist.
- Hovering a page title surfaces page-level actions like `Set property`.
- Deleting a page moves it to a **Recycle** bin for 30 days — recreate the page or restore it from the three-dots menu. (Tag pages, property pages, and today's journal delete permanently.)

## What's still block-only

- Blocks live inside a page and have a position in the outline.
- Block names don't have to be unique.
- Blocks can have reactions — right-click the bullet → `Add reaction`.

## Block ↔ page conversion

A top-level block can become a page and back again:

- Tag the block `#Page` — it becomes a page, namespaced under its parent.
- To turn it back, press `p t` and remove `#Page`.

Handy when a stray note grows into something that deserves its own page.
